# Artificial Intelligence — Concepts and Overview

## What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. These tasks include learning, reasoning, problem-solving, perception, language understanding, and decision-making.

## Types of AI

### Narrow AI (Weak AI)
Narrow AI is designed for specific tasks. Examples include virtual assistants (Siri, Alexa), image recognition systems, and recommendation engines. Most current AI systems are narrow AI.

### General AI (Strong AI)
General AI would have human-level intelligence and could perform any intellectual task. This remains a theoretical concept and has not been achieved.

### Super AI
Super AI would surpass human intelligence. This is purely hypothetical and the subject of ongoing research and debate.

## Machine Learning

Machine Learning (ML) is a subset of AI where systems learn from data without being explicitly programmed. Key types include:

- **Supervised Learning**: Learning from labelled data (e.g., classification, regression)
- **Unsupervised Learning**: Finding patterns in unlabelled data (e.g., clustering)
- **Reinforcement Learning**: Learning through trial and error with rewards

## Deep Learning

Deep Learning uses neural networks with many layers to learn complex patterns. It excels at:
- Image and speech recognition
- Natural language processing
- Game playing (AlphaGo)
- Generative AI (ChatGPT, Gemini, DALL-E)

## Natural Language Processing (NLP)

NLP enables computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language. Key tasks include:
- **Sentiment Analysis**: Determining the emotional tone of text
- **Named Entity Recognition**: Identifying people, places, and organisations
- **Intent Classification**: Understanding user purpose
- **Machine Translation**: Converting text between languages
- **Text Summarisation**: Creating concise summaries

## Large Language Models (LLMs)

LLMs like GPT, Gemini, Claude, and LLaMA are trained on vast text datasets and can:
- Generate human-like text
- Answer questions
- Translate languages
- Write code
- Summarise documents
- Engage in conversation

They work by predicting the next token in a sequence based on patterns learned during training.

## Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

RAG combines the power of LLMs with external knowledge retrieval:
1. User asks a question
2. Relevant documents are retrieved from a knowledge base
3. The retrieved context is provided to the LLM
4. The LLM generates a grounded, accurate response

This reduces hallucination and allows the AI to access up-to-date information.

## AI Ethics and Safety

Important considerations in AI development:
- **Bias**: AI systems can reflect biases in training data
- **Transparency**: Users should know when they're interacting with AI
- **Privacy**: Personal data must be handled responsibly
- **Safety**: AI should not be used for harmful purposes
- **Accountability**: There should be human oversight of AI decisions
